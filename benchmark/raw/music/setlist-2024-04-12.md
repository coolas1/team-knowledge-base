# Polyp live — Small Quiet Rooms, 2024-04-12

> First public performance of the Tidal material. 35-minute set, no break.
> Venue: Small Quiet Rooms, 1824 San Pablo Ave, Berkeley. ~60 capacity,
> seated. The crowd was mostly label regulars plus three of Wren's labmates
> (Maria, Priya, Daichi) who drove down from Santa Cruz. Reflection in
> [journal/2024-05-08](../journal/2024-05-08.md).

## Rig

- Ableton Live 12 on a 14" MacBook Pro, audio interface: MOTU M4
- Hardware: Elektron Digitone (pads), Digitakt (rhythm/loops), Korg MS-20
  (sub-bass on tracks 2, 3, 5)
- Monitoring: venue provided JBL 705P + a single sub; I added a ButtKicker
  on the stage floor for the sub-bass drops on track 3
- Hydrophone feed: live signal from a small tank of seawater set up beside
  the stage (Wren's idea — see [field-recordings-okinawa.md](field-recordings-okinawa.md)
  Recording 1 for the source inspiration)

## Notes for next time

1. **Sub-bass monitoring**: the ButtKicker helped on stage, but the audience
   zone (rows 4–6) had a noticeable null. Next time, two subs at the front.
2. **Field-rec bed timing**: the live hydrophone was hard to mix against
   the Digitone pads. Consider a dedicated aux channel next time with a
   slow compressor (4:1, 200 ms attack) to keep it present without
   overwhelming.
3. **Visuals**: none this time, which was the right call for the room — but
   "Cladocopium" would benefit from projected footage of site RYK-06. Need
   to source that footage during the next fieldwork trip.

## Reception

Audience response was warmer than expected. Two people asked about the
whale song in track 6 (it's real — see [polyp-tidal-ep-notes.md](polyp-tidal-ep-notes.md)
§T5). One person recognized the bottling-line loop as awamori-related,
which felt like the highest-compliment moment of the night.
