## The big one: resilience as oversampling diversity

**Pulls from**: [research/coral-resilience-paper.md](../research/coral-resilience-paper.md)
§3.3, [music/field-recordings-okinawa.md](../music/field-recordings-okinawa.md)
Recording 1 vs Recording 3, [fermentation/recipes.md](../fermentation/recipes.md)
koji section.

**The spark**: a coral reef is more resilient when its symbiont community
is more diverse than it strictly needs to be at any given moment (the
"rare biosphere" hypothesis, Boulotte 2016). A soundscape is more
"resilient" — in the sense of acoustically interesting, not in any formal
sense — when it has more layers than the human ear can resolve at once
(Schafer 1977). A koji culture is more robust when its microbiome is more
diverse than just the target *Aspergillus oryzae*.

**Where this could go**: a paper arguing that the same mathematical
structure (high-tail diversity conferring buffer capacity) shows up in
ecological, acoustic, and microbial systems. Probably overreach. But
worth a perspective piece. Started in [journal/2024-05-08](../journal/2024-05-08.md).

## Idea: reef soundscapes as a remote-sensing modality

**Pulls from**: [music/field-recordings-okinawa.md](../music/field-recordings-okinawa.md)
§"Sesoko West hydrophone II", [research/lab-notebook.md](../research/lab-notebook.md).

**The spark**: I have ~40 hours of paired hydrophone + quadrat data from
Sesoko West. The acoustic difference between March and April is audible
to a human ear. If it's also measurable, you could potentially use
hydrophones as a cheap proxy for bleaching — replace 4 hours of dive
time with a 5-minute drop.

**Where this could go**: a small grant proposal to NOAA Coral Reef Watch.
Probably 2025.

## Idea: koji cold-climate vs warm-climate strain comparison

**Pulls from**: [fermentation/recipes.md](../fermentation/recipes.md) koji
section, [travel/okinawa-2024/blog.md](../travel/okinawa-2024/blog.md)
"The jars" section.

**The spark**: the Nago koji-kin (warm-climate-adapted, ~80 years) is
faster and more aromatic than the Cold Mountain strain I'd been using.

amylase / protease expression.

**Where this could go**: probably just a [fermentation/batch-log.xlsx](../fermentation/batch-log.xlsx)
note rather than a paper. But if I could find a microbiologist collaborator
at UC Davis's food science program, maybe a small writeup.

## Idea: a Polyp album entirely from one site

**Pulls from**: [music/polyp-tidal-ep-notes.md](../music/polyp-tidal-ep-notes.md),
[music/field-recordings-okinawa.md](../music/field-recordings-okinawa.md).

site made audible.

## Idea: a paper on the "resilience refugia corridor"

**Pulls from**: [research/coral-resilience-paper.md](../research/coral-resilience-paper.md)
§4 Discussion, [research/lab-notebook.md](../research/lab-notebook.md)
"Open questions" #2.

**The spark**: Cape Hedo and Sesoko West are larval sources for the
central Ryukyus (this is the dispersal-corridor question). If we can
establish that, conservation priorities shift toward protecting those
two sites as biological reservoirs. Big modeling project.

**Where this could go**: the next first-author paper. Probably a
collaboration with the NOAA biophysical dispersal modelers.

## Idea: miso as a long-term dataset

**Pulls from**: [fermentation/recipes.md](../fermentation/recipes.md)
miso section, [journal/2024-08-22](../journal/2024-08-22.md).

**The spark**: a miso crock is essentially a low-frequency time series.
Start one every January for ten years; taste quarterly; write up the
trajectory. It's the kind of project that has no intellectual justification
and is therefore worth doing.

**Where this could go**: crock #1 already running (B-019). Crock #2 starts

## Idea: a trilingual reef-resilience perspective

**Pulls from**: [research/coral-resilience-paper.md](../research/coral-resilience-paper.md)
§4 Discussion, [research/south-china-sea-coral-zh.md](../research/south-china-sea-coral-zh.md),
[research/sesoko-field-notes-ja.md](../research/sesoko-field-notes-ja.md).

**The spark**: the Ryukyu dataset (DHW breakpoint 8.36) and Lin Wei's South
China Sea dataset (breakpoint 7.8) together span a real latitude gradient. A
perspective piece comparing resilience mechanisms across the West Pacific could
argue that lower-latitude reefs are more sensitive to cumulative heat stress.
I'd draft it in English but keep a Japanese and a Chinese abstract.

**Where this could go**: a perspective/comment in *Global Change Biology*. The
trilingual vocabulary is already started in
[notes/multilingual-glossary.md](multilingual-glossary.md); the Mandarin study
log is [notes/mandarin-study-zh.md](mandarin-study-zh.md). Probably 2025.
