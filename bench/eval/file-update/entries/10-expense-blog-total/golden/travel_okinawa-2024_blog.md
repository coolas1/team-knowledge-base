# Six weeks between reefs and jars

> **Okinawa, March 1 – April 19, 2024.** Six weeks. Three threads (research,
> music, fermentation) braided tighter than I expected. This post is the
> long-form version; daily plans in [itinerary.md](itinerary.md), every yen
> accounted for in [expenses.csv](expenses.csv). Reflection a month later
> at [journal/2024-05-08](../../journal/2024-05-08.md).

## Why

I'd been planning this trip since the 2023 heatwave. Three things pulled me
back to the Ryukyus at the same time:

1. **The data**: the 2023 numbers from Cape Hedo and Sesoko West were
   extraordinary — see [research/lab-notebook.md](../../research/lab-notebook.md)
   2023-08-14 — and I needed a second quarter of in-situ observations to
   convince *Coral Reefs* that the resilience signal was real.
2. **The recordings**: I'd been sketching the second Polyp EP since November
   (see [music/polyp-tidal-ep-notes.md](../../music/polyp-tidal-ep-notes.md)),
   and the Ryukyus are the best subtropical soundscape I know.
3. **The ferments**: koji and awamori are older here than anywhere else in
   Japan, and a sabbatical meant time to actually visit breweries.

## The reefs (research thread)

Across the six weeks I logged 28 dives at the twelve sites in
[research/dive-sites.csv](../../research/dive-sites.csv). The pattern from
2023 held: Cape Hedo (RYK-05) and Sesoko West (RYK-06) stayed under 20%
bleaching while sites to the south exceeded 60%. The full site-level summary
is in [research/bleaching-survey-2024.xlsx](../../research/bleaching-survey-2024.xlsx).

Photos from two of the dives (Yonaguni West, RYK-01 — the drift dive that
became the source of the "Tidal II" recording):
[dive-1.jpg](photos/dive-1.jpg) and
[dive-2.jpg](photos/dive-2.jpg). The reef panorama is from a shallower

The hydrophone recordings are transcribed in
[music/field-recordings-okinawa.md](../../music/field-recordings-okinawa.md).
The most striking thing about Recording 3 (Sesoko West in April, mid-heatwave)
is how *quieter* the reef sounded than the same site in March. Snapping-shrimp
crackle was visibly sparser. Whether that's a stress signal or just seasonal
variance is a question for the next paper.

## The jars (fermentation thread)

Three things came home with me:

- A bag of *Aspergillus oryzae* spores (koji-kin) from a small brewer outside
  Nago. Same strain I'd been buying from Cold Mountain in the US, but the
  brewer insisted the Okinawan selection had been warm-climate adapted for
  ~80 years. First test batch (B-018 in
  [batch-log.xlsx](../../fermentation/batch-log.xlsx)) confirmed it: faster
  bloom at 30 °C, more chestnut in the aroma.
- A bottle of 8-year-aged Kuusu Misaki from the Zuisen distillery in Itoman.
  Wild — it's aged in clay under the ocean floor.
- A renewed conviction that my koji process is under-salted; see the
  revised miso recipe in [fermentation/recipes.md](../../fermentation/recipes.md).

## What it cost

## What I'd do differently

1. **Two weeks less in Naha, two more on Ishigaki.** The Naha food scene
   is great but the diving there is the weakest in the archipelago.
2. **Earlier hydrophone deployments.** I lost three days to a battery issue
   on the Zoom H5. Pack a backup battery next time.
3. **Visit at least one shochu distillery on the mainland before the next
   trip.** The awamori tradition is great, but I realized mid-trip that I
   don't know enough about the Kagoshima sweet-potato shochu lineage to
   compare.
