# A22 — Tidal album cover visual design

## Answer

The cover is an **800 × 800** PNG with three layers:

### (a) Background — vertical navy → teal gradient

Top of canvas: dark navy (approximately RGB **(10, 31, 61)**).
Bottom of canvas: teal (approximately RGB **(26, 107, 122)**). The
transition is a linear vertical interpolation between the two.
A subtle ~2% pixel-grain noise is overlaid (random ±22 per channel).

### (b) Central graphic — waveform of 60 vertical white bars

A horizontal waveform sits slightly **below center** (centerline at y ≈ 480
on the 800-tall canvas). It is made of **60 thin vertical white bars**
(each bar 6 px wide) with varying heights sampled from a sine wave
(`sin(i * 0.42)`) plus uniform random jitter. Bar alpha is ~230/255
(near-opaque white). The waveform visually evokes an audio amplitude
trace — appropriate for an ambient-music EP.

### (c) Text — three centered lines

1. **"TIDAL"** — large (font size 74), bold, white. Y ≈ 180.
2. **"POLYP"** — medium (font size 30), bold, light blue-gray
   (RGB **(210, 220, 230)**). Y ≈ 270.
3. **"2024  ·  FIELD RECORDINGS"** — small (font size 14), bold, dimmer
   gray-blue (RGB **(170, 180, 190)**). Y ≈ 720. Note the middle-dot
   separator (·, U+00B7) with a double space on each side.

Font is **DejaVuSans-Bold** throughout.

## Gotchas

- The Picsum-downloaded photos elsewhere in the corpus (e.g.,
  `scoby-jar.jpg`, `dive-1.jpg`) are *generic stock images* whose pixel
  content is arbitrary. **This album cover is different** — it's a
  fully-procedural PIL image, so the visual content is fully knowable
  from the generator source. Don't treat it as unpredictable like the
  Picsum images.
- The middle-dot in the bottom text line is sometimes misread by OCR as
  a bullet (•) or a regular period (.). The actual character is U+00B7.
