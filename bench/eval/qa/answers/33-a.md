# A33 — How Wren's hongqu process differs from her koji process

## Answer

- **Microbe**: hongqu uses 红曲霉 ***Monascus purpureus***; Japanese koji uses
  黄曲霉 ***Aspergillus oryzae*** (麹). Different fungi entirely.
- **Optimum temperature**: hongqu is warmer, **~32–35 °C** (Wren ran hers near
  33 °C); koji is cooler, **~28–32 °C**.

## Sources

- `raw/fermentation/hongqu-notes-zh.md` (Chinese) — names *M. purpureus* and the
  32–35 °C optimum.
- `raw/fermentation/recipes.md` (English) — koji section, 28–32 °C range.
- `raw/fermentation/hongqu-ph-readings-zh.csv` — mash temperatures hover near 33 °C.

## Reasoning

1. The Chinese hongqu note gives the microbe (*M. purpureus*) and 32–35 °C.
2. The English recipes note gives koji's microbe (*A. oryzae*) and 28–32 °C.
3. Bridging the two: same substrate (rice), different fungus, hongqu ~3–5 °C warmer.

## Gotchas

- Don't conflate 红曲/hongqu (Monascus) with 麹/koji (Aspergillus) — the names
  look related but the microbes differ.
